# Phase I7 Claim-to-Evidence Matrix (Revised)

**Research Scope**: Evaluation of Reader-Writer Synchronization Architectures in Go Log-Structured Merge Trees  
**Architecture Under Investigation**:
- **Approach A**: Coarse-Grained `sync.RWMutex` spanning in-memory search and physical disk I/O.
- **Approach A-min**: Minimal Reader Descriptor Leasing with Short Critical Section and Retired Reader Tracking.
- **Approach B**: Immutable Reader Snapshot Pinning (`SuperVersion`-style) with Atomic Pointer Swap and Deferred Epoch Reclamation.

**Experimental Execution Structure**:
- **Phase I (Historical WSL2/Linux ext4)**: 960 independent trials ($N = 10$ per cell across 6 workloads and 8 reader concurrencies up to $r = 32$) comparing Approach A vs. Approach B on ext4 on WSL2 Ubuntu 24.04.
- **Phase II (Windows 11 / NTFS Native, Phase I7)**: 60 prospective trials across 7 blocks ($N = 3$ matched triplets per block) evaluating Approach A, Approach A-min, and Approach B on bare-metal Windows 11 NTFS (Samsung NVMe SSD).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Methodological Boundary**: Phase I (WSL2/ext4) and Phase II (Windows/NTFS) constitute distinct, non-pooled experimental phases with different operating system kernels, filesystem drivers, write-pacing harnesses, and synchronization variants. Their performance ratios are never pooled into a single statistical distribution.

## 2. Inadmissible Claims & Explicit Non-Claims

The following claims are **empirically unsupported or methodologically invalid** and are strictly excluded from the manuscript:

1. **"Complete Elimination of Writer Starvation"**: Refuted by finite lock acquisition windows. Approach A-min and Approach B substantially mitigate writer starvation, but writers still synchronize on brief metadata swaps.
2. **"Strict Mathematical Equivalence"**: Never claim that failure to reject a null hypothesis ($p > 0.05$) proves exact mathematical equivalence. Equivalence is bounded strictly within the empirical $[-5\%, +5\%]$ equivalence band over 18 tested triplets.
3. **"Universal Superiority of Snapshot Pinning"**: Refuted by Phase I Workload D, where snapshot allocation and atomic reference counting caused a 26%–37% throughput regression at $r \le 4$.
4. **"Pebble Comparative Performance"**: Excluded because `pebble_runner.exe` suffered from envelope schema incompatibility (`INVALID`) and buffer exhaustion that dropped over 10.45 million latency samples (>50% of trial operations).
5. **"Exhaustive All-Miss Multi-Level Scan Verification"**: Excluded because Block E5 absent-key generation produced keys strictly exceeding the populated keyspace, causing SSTable binary searches to truncate immediately at the final data block index.
6. **"Long-Term Campaign Drift Invariance"**: Excluded because Block D3 startup trials failed on launch ($N_{\text{start}} = 0$), rendering the preregistered drift ratio test unavailable.
7. **"Cross-Phase Pooled Statistics"**: Pooling Phase I (WSL2/ext4) and Phase II (Windows/NTFS) metrics is strictly prohibited; they represent distinct experimental systems and environments.
8. **"Novelty of Basic Synchronization Primitives"**: Reference-counted readers, immutable version arrays, and reader leasing are established techniques in database and operating system literature (e.g., LevelDB, RocksDB, RCU); the paper's contribution is the controlled architectural dissection and comparison of leasing vs. snapshotting in Go.
